# decodo vs iproyal: Compare residential proxy pricing, sessions, and the better fit for your workload

Choosing between Decodo and IPRoyal gets confusing fast because both sell several proxy types, both support common protocols, and both advertise large residential networks. The useful question is not which dashboard looks nicer. It is whether you need rotating residential traffic, long-lived sessions, a scraping stack, or simply fixed ISP IPs with no bandwidth meter ticking in the background.

For most people comparing **Decodo vs IPRoyal**, the decision comes down to four things:

- How predictable your monthly traffic volume is
- Whether unused residential bandwidth needs to remain available
- How much location and session control your workflow needs
- Whether static ISP proxies would actually make more sense than rotating residential traffic

Decodo is generally the more complete data-collection platform. IPRoyal is often easier to justify for uneven workloads because its residential traffic does not expire. HypeProxies belongs in the conversation when the actual requirement is stable U.S. ISP IPs, unlimited bandwidth, and a fixed monthly bill rather than a rotating residential pool.

> Residential proxies are billed by traffic. Static ISP proxies are usually billed per IP. Comparing only the “starting price” without checking the billing model is how a cheap proxy plan becomes an expensive surprise.

## Quick verdict: Decodo or IPRoyal?

**Choose Decodo if** you run recurring scraping, price monitoring, market research, or multi-account workflows and want a larger advertised residential pool, granular targeting, and access to a broader scraping-tool ecosystem. Its public residential subscriptions start at 3 GB and get cheaper per GB as volume increases.

**Choose IPRoyal if** your residential traffic is irregular, you want to buy a small amount without a recurring minimum, or non-expiring traffic matters more than a lower monthly subscription rate. It offers rotating and sticky sessions, city-level targeting, and an advertised 64M+ residential IP pool.

**Consider HypeProxies instead if** you do not need rotating residential IPs at all. Its current public plans are static U.S. ISP proxy packages with unlimited bandwidth and unlimited threads. That is a fundamentally different purchase: fewer fixed IPs, but stable sessions and no per-GB accounting.

The short version: Decodo is usually the better all-round platform for regular web-data operations; IPRoyal is compelling for flexible, non-expiring residential usage; HypeProxies fits workloads that need persistent U.S. ISP identities rather than a large rotating network.

## Residential proxies: where Decodo has the edge

Decodo’s strongest case is the combination of residential scale and built-in data-collection products. The platform advertises 115M+ ethically sourced residential IPs across 195+ locations, including targeting at continent, country, state, city, ZIP code, and ASN level.

That targeting depth is useful for legitimate tasks where the result needs a specific local viewpoint: checking a country-specific product listing, monitoring public search results by city, validating localized ad delivery, or collecting publicly accessible regional pricing data.

The platform also offers more than raw proxy access. Its broader catalog includes web-scraping APIs, templates, tools for structured extraction, and developer integrations. That matters when the job is not merely “send requests through an IP.” A team that would otherwise assemble separate proxy, rendering, parser, and anti-blocking services may find the integrated route simpler.

Decodo’s current pricing page lists a 99.92% success rate, 99.99% uptime, and average response time below 0.5 seconds for its residential product. These are provider-reported figures, so treat them as directional rather than a guarantee for your specific targets. A successful request to a simple public page and a successful request to a heavily protected marketplace are not comparable tests.

Decodo is the sensible choice when:

- You expect steady monthly residential traffic.
- You need detailed geographic targeting.
- You want both rotating and sticky sessions.
- You are building a data workflow and value APIs or scraping tools alongside proxies.
- You want the lower public per-GB rate at small-to-medium monthly subscription volumes.

## Residential proxies: where IPRoyal makes more sense

IPRoyal’s advantage is flexibility. Its residential product advertises 64M+ genuine residential IP addresses, global coverage, unlimited concurrent sessions, and both rotating and sticky configurations. Its documentation describes rotation controls ranging from per-request changes to session time-to-live settings that can extend up to seven days.

That longer sticky-session range can be especially relevant for legitimate multi-step workflows. Think of a public site that sets a session cookie and expects the same connection identity across multiple pages. Rotating too quickly in that situation can create more problems than it solves.

IPRoyal also offers the option to change an IP through the dashboard or API, and it describes timed auto-rotation intervals of 1, 10, or 30 minutes. Those controls make it easier to match a proxy configuration to a workflow instead of treating “residential” as one generic product.

The more important commercial distinction is non-expiring traffic. If you run research projects irregularly, that can reduce waste. There is no prize for consuming a monthly proxy allowance merely because the calendar is about to turn over.

IPRoyal is the better fit when:

- Your traffic demand is uneven or project-based.
- You need to keep purchased residential bandwidth available for future use.
- You want fine-grained sticky-session timing.
- You only need a small test amount before committing.
- You primarily need proxy infrastructure rather than an all-in-one scraping platform.

One caveat: non-expiring traffic does not automatically make IPRoyal cheaper. If you burn through traffic every month, Decodo’s lower subscription pricing can still come out ahead.

## Session control is more important than many comparisons admit

“Rotating residential proxy” is not a complete requirement. You need to decide whether your task wants a new IP often, the same IP briefly, or the same IP for a long time.

### Use rotating sessions for broad public-data collection

Rotation can make sense for requests that are independent of one another: checking a large set of public pages, gathering public product prices, or collecting regional search results. The goal is not to hold a particular identity; it is to distribute legitimate traffic responsibly and avoid concentrating every request on one endpoint.

Both Decodo and IPRoyal offer rotating residential access.

### Use sticky sessions for multi-step browsing

A sticky session preserves the same exit IP for a defined period. This is useful when a legitimate workflow has state: public browsing across several pages, maintaining a session cookie, or testing a region-specific experience over a short sequence of pages.

Decodo lists rotating and sticky sessions. IPRoyal advertises session settings ranging from one second to seven days, which gives it a clearer edge if extended session persistence is central to the workflow.

### Use static ISP proxies when the identity itself matters

A static ISP proxy is a fixed IP assigned for a longer period. It is often a better fit for stable sessions, account management performed in accordance with the destination platform’s rules, or workflows where bandwidth volume is high but the required number of identities is modest.

This is where comparing Decodo and IPRoyal residential plans may be asking the wrong question. A per-GB rotating plan can become awkward when the real requirement is “keep these 50 U.S. IPs stable all month.”

## Common mistakes when choosing between Decodo and IPRoyal

### Buying a rotating plan for a static-session job

If you need stable identities all day, the cheapest rotating GB plan may be a false economy. Start by asking whether static ISP IPs are the correct proxy type.

### Looking only at the lowest advertised rate

IPRoyal’s bulk rate and Decodo’s $2/GB high-volume enterprise starting rate are not the price a one-GB buyer will pay. Compare the tier you will actually purchase.

### Ignoring unused traffic rules

A lower recurring price is not necessarily better if you cannot consume the included bandwidth. For irregular work, IPRoyal’s non-expiring traffic can change the calculation.

### Treating country targeting as guaranteed city coverage

Both services advertise broad global coverage, but supply fluctuates. Check your specific location requirement during a trial or before a major commitment.

### Assuming a proxy removes every block

It does not. Responsible request rates, correct session handling, public-data scope, and adherence to each destination’s terms still matter. Proxies are infrastructure, not a loophole.
